# Go to Market

How Remittance Rail would reach its first users and grow. Targets here are described qualitatively. Specific numbers will be set from pilot data, not guessed in advance.

## Who we serve first

- **Senders:** migrant workers in Singapore, such as domestic, construction, service and shipyard workers, who send money to the Philippines regularly.
- **Receivers:** their families, including those far from banks.
- **Partners:** a licensed Singapore transfer entity, an on-ramp, and Philippines cash-out agents and wallets.

## Why start with Singapore to Philippines

- Large, active worker communities and regular, repeat transfers.
- Widespread mobile wallet use in the Philippines.
- Singapore is a hub for regulated digital-payment innovation, which helps with licensing conversations.

## Positioning

**More of every paycheck reaches the family it was meant for, with every cent in plain sight.**

We compete on three things workers can feel: a lower and fully shown cost, faster delivery, and a receipt anyone can check. We do not ask users to understand crypto.

## How we reach users

1. **Employers and employer groups.** Partner with employers and dormitory or community operators who already help workers with money matters.
2. **NGOs and worker associations.** Migrant-worker NGOs are trusted voices and can run onboarding sessions in the workers' own languages.
3. **Word of mouth in communities.** Workers trust other workers. The pilot starts with an invited group and grows through referrals.
4. **Cash-out agents.** Agents in the Philippines tell families about the service at the point of collection.

## Onboarding approach

- A familiar, mobile-first app in the user's language.
- Agent-assisted and community-assisted first transfers.
- A first transfer that shows the full cost and the receiver amount before confirming, to build trust.

## Pricing approach

- A small, transparent per-transfer fee, set below the corridor average once pilot data confirms the all-in cost.
- Revenue share with cash-out and on-ramp partners.
- The fee is always shown before the sender confirms. No hidden exchange-rate margin.

## Launch plan

| Stage | Focus |
|---|---|
| Before launch | Sign a licensed partner and a cash-out partner. Run user interviews. Prepare local-language materials. |
| Closed pilot | A small invited group of workers. Weekly feedback. Measure cost, speed and collection for every transfer. |
| Public launch in the corridor | Open to more workers through employers, NGOs and referrals. Add agents. |
| Expansion | Add Indonesia, India and Bangladesh, using the same rails and the same playbook. |

## Partnerships we need

- A licensed Singapore transfer entity.
- A stablecoin on-ramp and off-ramp provider.
- A Philippines cash-out partner (mobile wallet, agent network, or both).
- A compliance advisor.
- One or two employer or NGO partners for first users.

## Metrics we will track

- All-in cost versus the corridor average.
- Send-to-cash-out time.
- Share of transfers collected successfully.
- Money saved per user per year.
- Agents onboarded.
- Repeat usage, as a sign workers trust it.

## Competition and differentiation

Existing remittance services are the real alternative, and some are already fast. Our difference is a fully shown cost and rate before sending, escrow with automatic refund, a public receipt, and open rails where any licensed agent can compete. We will name specific competitors and compare honestly with verified data during Phase 0.

## Main go-to-market risks

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Workers do not trust a new service | Start through employers, NGOs and word of mouth, with agent-assisted onboarding |
| Ramp costs erase the savings | Negotiate partner rates and publish the all-in cost, even if it is not a win |
| Licensing delays | Use a licensed partner and confirm the path in Phase 0 before building |
| Agents are slow to join | Start with one partner and make collection simple and fairly paid |
